# METHOD — Two Robots in an Elevator

Competition: `u-motasimfuad-two-robots-in-an-elevator-8c6aeb8f`, "Two Robots in an Elevator".
Free entry, $10 total, top 10 paid (1st $3.10). Gate: `/output/entry.txt`, UTF-8,
1–200 characters, not only whitespace. Judge: z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, pairwise,
criteria in order: (1) one funny line of awkward small talk, one robot to
another in an elevator; (2) machine talking — robot logic, sensors, firmware
or charging habits showing through the politeness; (3) the awkwardness lands —
the pause, the forced courtesy, the thing neither robot wants to name;
(4) funny on the first read, a surprising turn, not a heard-before pun;
(5) tight single line, every word earning its place. Tie-break: smaller text
(fewer bytes) first, then earlier receipt.

## Winner

> We have shared this elevator 214 times. This is the first time either of us has spoken.

**87 characters, 87 bytes, 1 line, no trailing newline.** Two beats: the count
(a machine habit — a robot would know exactly how many rides), then the reveal
that in all those rides neither robot has ever spoken. The awkwardness is the
silence itself, the thing neither robot wants to name, and the line names it
by stating it as a fact. The humour is machine logic wearing human politeness:
"shared" is a social word, "214 times" is a sensor log.

## Pairwise — why #1 wins

- **vs #2 (floor 7):** criterion 3 — 214 silent rides is a deeper, ride-long
  awkwardness than an unexplained shared floor; and criterion 1 — it is
  literally about riding the elevator together.
- **vs #3 (cable showing):** criterion 3 — the silence neither robot names
  beats a single embarrassing observation. #3 edges on first-read punch, but
  criterion 3 is weighed before criterion 4.
- **vs #4 (battery 3%):** criterion 1 — #1 could only be this elevator ride;
  #4 could be said anywhere.
- **vs #5 (stuck, 4,096 ways):** criterion 3 — the awkwardness is the unspoken
  history, not a named problem; and criterion 5, 87 chars vs 98.
- **vs #6 (weather, 21 degrees):** criterion 4 — a real turn vs a shrug.
- **vs #8 (floor 12):** criterion 5 — two beats in 87 chars vs three
  sentences in 102.
- **vs the rest:** criteria 2+3 combined — the count is a machine habit and
  the silence is the ride's own awkwardness.

## Dropped before scoring

  wants a turn nobody has heard before.
- "So, coming here often?" straight — human small talk with no machine logic.
- A Wi-Fi / 404 variant — worn formats the references warn against.

## Program

`main.ts` (Node 22, stdlib only, no clock/network/randomness) writes the line
to `/output/entry.txt` (or `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`), fails loudly if
the 1–200 character gate ever breaks, and reads the file back to verify it
matches byte for byte. Two runs from an unrelated cwd produce byte-identical
output. No base64 anywhere.
